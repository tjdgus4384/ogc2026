% Abstract
% =================================================
\begin{abstract}
The challenge asks for a joint spatial and temporal plan for shipyard blocks:
every block must receive a bay, an orientation, a position, and an entry day so
that a weighted sum of total tardiness ($Z_1$), workload imbalance ($Z_2$), and
bay-preference regret ($Z_3$) is minimized under exact polygonal, multi-layer,
and crane-accessibility feasibility rules. Our solver couples a compiled C++
engine with a defensive Python driver. The engine implements a conservative
feasibility oracle that reproduces the official checker's five rules, and runs
a four-thread portfolio of adaptive large-neighborhood search (ALNS) annealers
whose destroy operators include priced, goal-directed ejection chains gated by
dimensionless instance predicates. Cheap atomic moves --- slot swaps,
in-place shifts, and score-neutral spatial compaction --- attack the measured
failure mode of dense boards, geometric fragmentation. A memetic population
loop with consensus-locking crossover serves tight-budget instances, and the
tail of the time limit is split adaptively between annealing restarts and an
exact CP-SAT re-placement subsolver made viable by engine-side pair-conflict
construction. All schedules are scale-invariant in the block count, every
returned solution passes the official checker, and a construction-feasible
fallback guarantees a valid answer under any failure. On the public evaluation
instances the solver attains objectives of 80{,}145 to 29.1M at official time
limits with zero infeasibilities or overruns, and it remains feasible on all
40 final-round training instances.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Challenge Problem \& Main Difficulties}

Each block $i$ carries a release day $R_i$, a processing time $P_i$, a due day
$D_i$, a workload $L_i$, bay preference scores $S_{ij}$, and a shape given by
$K_i$ stacked polygonal layers. A solution assigns to every block a bay, one
of its allowed orientations, an integer position $(x,y)$, and an entry day
$e_i$; the block exits at $f_i = e_i + P_i$. The objective is
$w_1 Z_1 + w_2 Z_2 + w_3 Z_3$, where $Z_1$ is total tardiness
$\sum_i \max(0, f_i - D_i)$, $Z_2$ is the maximum area-normalized workload
imbalance over bays and days, and $Z_3$ is the preference regret
$\sum_i (S_i^{\max} - S_{i,j(i)})$.

Three properties make the problem hard. First, feasibility is genuinely
geometric: blocks are non-convex polygons stacked in layers, two blocks
conflict only when time-overlapping same-layer polygons intersect with
positive area, and a crane rule requires the moving block's layer to clear
\emph{all} layers at or above its settling level along a vertical descent, at
both entry and exit. Bounding-box reasoning is unsound in both directions: it
forbids legal interlocked placements and cannot express the asymmetric crane
relation. Second, the objective mixes two regimes. On some instances tardiness
dominates by an order of magnitude; on others tardiness is zero and the game
is pure preference regret, so any fixed tuning for one regime fails the other.
Third, evaluation instances are hidden: constants tuned to visible sizes
silently change the algorithm's behavior at other scales, and every run must
return a feasible solution within a hard time limit.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A compiled, conservative feasibility oracle reproducing the five rules
of the official checker (same-layer overlap, crane entry, crane exit, and
non-blocking of later entries and exits), audited against the checker on
uniform, contact-tie, and nesting cases with zero discrepancies.
\item A four-thread portfolio ALNS with ten adaptive destroy operators,
including two \emph{priced} directed ejection operators (a preference-chain
and a tardiness min-cut ejection) whose activation is gated by dimensionless
predicates computed from the instance's own weights, not tuned thresholds.
\item Atomic moves that require no free space --- tardy-initiated slot swaps,
in-place shifts, and provably score-neutral spatial compaction --- derived
from a measured diagnosis that residual tardiness is caused by geometric
fragmentation rather than lack of capacity.
\item An adaptive tail allocator that splits the final fraction of the budget
between annealing restarts and an \emph{exact} CP-SAT re-placement subsolver;
engine-side pair-conflict construction accelerates the conflict model by
orders of magnitude and is what makes the exact arm affordable in the limit.
\item Scale-invariant search schedules (cooling, reheating, removal sizes,
stagnation windows all relative to $n$) and a defensive driver in which every
returned solution passes the official checker, with a construction-feasible
fallback for worst-case degradation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Algorithm Description}

\subsection{Overall Algorithm Framework}

The driver calls a single compiled engine through the following pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Seed construction.} Six insertion orders (earliest-due-date,
slack-based, noised variants) are replayed through the engine's greedy placer;
the best three distinct layouts become seeds. The placer performs
earliest-entry first-fit in three phases per block: a full corner scan at
$e = R_i$, a local scan around each residual block's exit event ascending in
time (space opens only when someone leaves), and a sparse dense sweep over the
remaining days as a completeness net.
\item \textbf{Portfolio search.} Four OpenMP threads anneal in parallel with
heterogeneous personas: removal-size multipliers, initial temperatures,
pure-annealing versus hill-climbing tails, operator subsets, and area-screen
regimes all differ, and the four starting points hedge between the best seed,
a fresh construction, and the runners-up. Acceptance is simulated annealing on
the exact objective; half of the personas switch to hill climbing in the last
quarter of their budget.
\item \textbf{Population path.} When the per-block budget is tight
($\mathrm{sec}/n \le 1.6$ and $\mathrm{sec} \ge 150$), a memetic outer loop
replaces the flat portfolio: distinct constructions are hardened by short
bursts into a founder population, and offspring are produced by
consensus-locking crossover --- only blocks whose bay assignment both elite
parents agree on are locked, the rest is re-annealed --- replacing the worst
member.
\item \textbf{Slice-refined tail.} The last 30\% of the budget is cut into
slices; each slice is given to whichever of two arms showed the higher
measured improvement rate per second in the previous slice: (a) portfolio
annealing restarted from the global best, or (b) an exact re-placement polish
that frees a random subset of $\approx 25$ blocks seeded by a tardy block and
re-places them \emph{optimally} against the fixed remainder with CP-SAT. The
tail is skipped when the population path is armed, and it always stops before
the checker gate's repair reserve.
\item \textbf{Assignment descent and gate.} A final descent accepts only
exactly-priced improving reassignments (with the assignment fixed, $Z_2$ and
$Z_3$ are closed-form and the checker rules close within bays). Every
candidate then passes the official checker; if repair cost exceeds a small
bound, runner-up candidates are auditioned through the same gate, and a
construction-feasible trivial solution guarantees a valid return in all
cases.
\end{enumerate}

Feasibility during search is decided by the engine's conservative oracle,
which evaluates the checker's five rules for a candidate block against the
residents of its bay. Polygon overlap follows the checker's semantics ---
proper edge intersection or strict-interior containment, with edge and vertex
contact allowed --- and the crane rules test the vertical descent of the
moving layer against all resident layers at or above the settling level, at
entry and at exit, including non-blocking of residents that enter or exit
during the block's stay. Same-day operations are ordered exits-first with
last-in-first-out exits, exactly as the checker replays them. Bounding boxes
and a bit-grid occupancy prefilter are used only as short-circuit
\emph{gates} in front of the exact tests, never as the decision.

\subsection{Key Ideas that Succeeded}

\textbf{Exact geometry as the oracle.} On dense boards the peak bay
occupancy is 103--105\% of bay area by bounding boxes but only 58--65\% by
true polygons: roughly a third of the usable capacity exists only through
interlocking, and a bounding-box oracle would forbid it. The oracle was
audited against the official checker on 2{,}000 uniform samples, 90{,}000
contact-tie cases, and 1{,}500 nesting cases with zero discrepancies.

\textbf{Priced, gated ejection operators.} Uniform random destruction cannot
compose the moves that matter on saturated boards. The preference-chain
operator empties a preferred bay only while the ejection cost stays below the
preference gain, then forces the mover in at its on-time entry
$e^\ast = \max(R_i, D_i - P_i)$; the tardiness min-cut operator scans
\emph{all} bays for the hole whose ejection set is cheapest inside a tardy
block's on-time window and removes exactly that set. Both are gated by
dimensionless predicates ($w_3 Z_3 > w_1 Z_1$; zero tardiness with small
regret) recomputed during search from the instance's own weights, so the
gating transfers to hidden instances by construction.

\textbf{Atomic moves against fragmentation.} Instrumented runs show the
repair step never fails to place a block (0 failures in over $10^6$
iterations per run); tardiness arises because the first feasible day is late.
Diagnostics attribute this to fragmentation: free area exists but is
scattered inside other blocks' bounding boxes, and no tardy block has any
feasible position in any bay during its release window. Three moves that need
no free space follow: slot swaps between a tardy block and a resident (2\% of
iterations), in-place shifts within a bay at fixed entry (10\%), and
end-of-round spatial compaction that pushes every resident to its leftmost
legal $x$. Since position does not enter the objective, compaction is
score-neutral by construction and purely manufactures contiguous space; it is
paired with a time compaction that only advances entries.

\textbf{Exact subsolver in the tail.} Re-placing small congested
neighborhoods exactly proves the boards locally optimal (gap 0.0\%), but
re-placing \emph{random} tardy-seeded subsets does improve them. The decisive
step was moving conflict construction into the engine: candidate-pair
conflicts (same-layer settling overlap plus vertical crane sweeps) for
$\sim$1{,}300 candidate placements --- about $10^5$ pairs --- are built in
under 0.1\,s, several hundred times faster than the interpreted equivalent,
which flips the cost-benefit sign of the exact arm inside the time limit.
Every CP-SAT proposal is re-verified by the engine's own oracle before
acceptance. Disabling the slice-refined tail worsens 26 of 40 final-round
training instances (median $+1.79\%$), with the largest losses on the largest
boards.

\textbf{Scale invariance.} Every schedule constant is expressed relative to
$n$: the cooling factor $\exp(-0.06/n)$ per iteration, reheating after $6n$
stagnant iterations, small removals of up to $\max(8,\, 0.03n)$ blocks,
large removals of $0.05n$ to $0.15n$ blocks, return to the incumbent after
$20n$ stagnant iterations, and a $1/n$-weighted slack surrogate. With
absolute constants the same code becomes a different algorithm at unseen
sizes.

\textbf{Measurement discipline.} Per-seed dispersion of 5--8\% (occasionally
25\%) makes single-run comparisons meaningless. Every adoption decision used
paired comparisons --- the same instances and seeds for both configurations
--- with a sign test over the 40 final-round training instances, and
refactorings were accepted only on byte-identical outputs at fixed iteration
counts. This discipline reversed one earlier adoption (see below).

\subsection{Key Ideas that Failed}

\emph{Heterogeneous acceptance.} Annealing half the portfolio with late
acceptance hill climbing looked profitable on six public instances but was
reversed by the 40-instance paired sign test (26 losses to 14 wins,
$p = 0.04$, mean $+2.34\%$); it was retracted. \emph{Smarter per-iteration
placement.} Best-of-$R$ repair, contact-score best-fit, and raster-NFP
tight packing all bought per-iteration quality at a larger cost in iteration
count and lost 1.5--8.4\% end to end; throughput governs quality.
\emph{Speed without throughput.} Two time-keyed candidate indexes cut loop
visits by 55--75\% and were byte-identical to the reference, yet gained
$\le 3\%$ and were rejected. \emph{Exact decomposition masters.} A
set-partitioning master over bay-plan columns (and a block-ownership
relaxation of it) closed to proven optimality with 0.0\% improvement ---
bay-plan recombination has no slack. A global placement MIP with tardiness
conflict cuts improved one preference-dominated instance marginally but did
not scale. \emph{Vacuous lower bound.} An area-relaxation bound closes to
$Z_1 = 0$ on all four public instances, yet its certificate assignment is not
geometrically realizable; the true optimality gap remains unknown.
\emph{Targeted room-making.} Forcing a mover into its preferred bay on time
and exactly re-placing the displaced neighbors works mechanically but never
paid for the displacement.

\subsection{Further Improvement Plan}

The measured residual gap sits in globally different geometric layouts, not
in local re-placement or scheduling. Three directions follow. (i) Replace the
quadratic pairwise conflict model with an exact sub-quadratic formulation so
the exact re-placement arm can scale from $\approx 25$ to $50$ or more
freed blocks. (ii) Recover the $\approx 14\%$ cross-thread final-value
dispersion of the portfolio with a temperature-preserving migration design;
naive reallocation schemes measurably fail. (iii) Density-gated
polygon-contact candidate generation, which wins on crowded boards and loses
on sparse ones, pending validation on enough boards to trust the gate on
hidden instances.

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:implementation}

The submission consists of a Python driver (\texttt{myalgorithm.py}, roughly
one thousand lines), a C++17 engine of roughly four thousand lines compiled
as a single translation unit (\texttt{src/api.cpp} including geometry, crane,
core, occupancy, repair, and search headers) into a pybind11 module, the
organizer-provided checker utilities (unmodified), and a build note with the
exact compile command and toolchain. The engine is built with
\texttt{g++ -O3} (C++17, OpenMP, \texttt{x86-64-v3},
\texttt{-ffp-contract=off}, LTO) for CPython 3.12, and a prebuilt binary for
the evaluation environment is included. Beyond the compiled extension, the
driver needs only the Python standard library; CP-SAT is used through
OR-Tools only when the interpreter already provides it.

The driver is defensive by design: the engine, the CP-SAT dependency, and the
checker are imported optionally, and every degradation path is closed --- if
CP-SAT is unavailable the exact arm silently drops and the annealing arm
keeps the tail; in the worst case a pure-Python, construction-feasible
trivial solution is returned. The entry point never raises. Threads are
capped at $\min(4, \mathrm{cpu\_count})$; no environment configuration is
read, so the evaluation environment exercises exactly the shipped defaults.
Peak memory stays below 600\,MB on the largest boards.

Determinism: within an iteration-governed round, the search trajectory is a
function of the seed and round count only; byte-identical results were
verified across nodes. Faster hardware packs more rounds into the limit, and
the best value is monotone in rounds, which is the basis for transferring
locally measured results to the evaluation server. Deadline safety is
enforced end to end: the tail stops before the checker gate's repair reserve,
and the gate itself has a bounded rescue ladder with runner-up audition.

The decomposition shows the two regimes explicitly: P3 is a pure
assignment game ($Z_1 = 0$; the objective is dominated by preference regret),
while on P4--P6 weighted tardiness contributes 76--97\% of the objective.
The dimensionless gates of Section~2 detect this split at run time; no
instance-specific configuration exists in the code.

On the 40 final-round training instances ($n \le 300$, $m \le 5$, weight
ratios $w_1/w_3$ spanning 4--107), the solver is feasible on all instances
with zero checker-gate failures and zero overruns, and the portfolio
sustains roughly $0.8$--$1.3 \times 10^6$ search iterations per run at
official limits. Under extended budgets the engine continues to convert time
into quality (8 of 10 large boards improve from 600\,s to 1{,}500\,s, up to
$-12.6\%$), indicating that the search does not freeze within the official
limits.

Honest limitations: the residual gap to the strongest known values on the
public instances is concentrated in tardiness. Local analysis proves our
boards exactly optimal for any re-placement of the 20--30 most congested
blocks. An earlier claim of time-global optimality at fixed geometry was too
strong: that timing model imposed $f_i=e_i+P_i$ and therefore excluded the
official $f_i-e_i\ge P_i$ crane-nesting freedom. A corrected exact-order audit
proves equality of the restricted and flexible optima on P3--P4, but remains
unresolved on P5--P6. Geometry and exit timing must therefore still be searched
jointly; the area-relaxation lower bound is vacuous, so the distance to true
optimality is unknown.

\section{AI Use Declaration}

Large-language-model coding assistants (Claude-family models used through an
agentic coding tool) were used throughout the project in three roles.
\emph{Code:} the assistant drafted and refactored large parts of the C++
engine and the Python driver under the participant's direction; all generated
code was reviewed by the participant and validated mechanically ---
byte-identity checks for refactorings, fixed-seed canaries, unit and battery
tests, and the official feasibility checker on every produced solution.
\emph{Experiments:} the assistant helped build the evaluation harnesses,
scheduled runs, and summarized measurements; experimental design, acceptance
criteria (paired sign tests at official time limits), and all
adopt-or-reject decisions were made by the participant, and several
assistant-proposed changes were rejected by these tests. \emph{Report:} the
assistant helped draft and edit this report from the project's measurement
records; the participant verified every quantitative claim against the
frozen logs and the submitted code and takes full responsibility for the
content. No AI system had autonomous decision authority over the submitted
algorithm or this report.
